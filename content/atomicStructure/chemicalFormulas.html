Chemical Symbols

Every type of atom on the periodic table has a unique chemical symbol.

  • These symbols always start with a capital letter.
  • If they have a second letter, it is always lowercase.

Chemical Formula

A chemical formula tells us what types of atoms (elements) are present in a chemical.

It also tells us how many of each type is present.

There are two types of chemical formulas:

  • Molecular Formulas
  • Formula Units

Molecular Formula

A molecular formula tells us what atoms are found in one molecule of a substance.

Some Examples: H2O, CO2, CH4

Molecular Formula

The molecular formula for caffeine (shown below) is: C8H10N4O2

Formula Units

Some compounds do not form molecules. Instead, they form giant crystal lattices.

A formula unit shows the simplest ratio of ions in an ionic compound.

Some examples: NaCl, KI, Ca(OH)2

Subscripts

If the same type of atom is present more than once we can use a subscript to indicate how many times it occurs.

A molecule of O2 contains two oxygen (O) atoms.

A molecule of BCl3 contains one Boron (B) and three chlorine (Cl) atoms.

A molecule of H2O contains 2 Hydrogen (H) atoms and one oxygen (O) atom.

Brackets

Some chemicals formula have brackets in them to group atoms together.

If there is a subscript after a bracket, it means that group of atoms is present more than once.

For example, in Ca(OH)2, there are two oxygens and two hydrogens.

In Fe(NO3)3, there are three nitrogens (N) and 9 oxygens (O) [multiply]
